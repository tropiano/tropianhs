## [Publishing][publishing]

So the big news is that I had to take down my bestseller. It was a voluntary decision, and there are many factors that determined it.
- Risky niche. My book was the first to copy the idea of a very famous puzzle book. The original book is in the top 100 in the US, and they have a huge budget. Since I don't think I have much differentiation yet, they might claim some kind of trademark on it. And this would mean the end of my account.
- My book was made focusing on speed at the expense of quality. In hindsight, it was not a great idea, but at the time I wanted to publish fast. The 1-star reviews started to come fast, and that really slowed down the launch and hurt the book long term too. Instead of trying to salvage it with extensive editing, taking it down and focusing on other projects looked like a better decision.  

I think I skipped July ad didn't publish anything this month. But I was working on a title that I am launching right now. It's in the word search space, another super-crowded niche. But I think I have a unique angle to succeed.  

My other books are all profitable. I am running ads and experimenting with them, and at the same time I am reviewing the listings and optimizing them. My ACOS in July has been 45%, up from 37% of June. It's not bad, but I gotta keep this under control, I have a tight budget and my cash needs to be saved for expensive Q4 ads.
